Examples from different settings

The same pattern, different settings.

The examples below are constructed to show what the difference between symbolic and real participation can look like. They do not describe any real organisations or events.

Note: All examples on this page are hypothetical. Any resemblance to real organisations is unintentional.

Constructed example 1

School

Symbolic participation

The student council is invited to give its views on a new school rule after the head teacher has already decided it. Two students get to appear in the photo in the school newsletter under the headline "the students have been involved".

Real participation

The student council is asked before the decision is made, gets time in the timetable to gather other students' views and gets a written answer on which proposals were included and why others were not.

Questions to ask

  • At what point in the process were the students asked?
  • Was there anything in the decision that could still be changed?
  • Were the students told what happened to their proposals?

Constructed example 2

Association or non-profit organisation

Symbolic participation

An association starts a youth group that is visible on social media and at the annual meeting. The group has no budget of its own and no seat on the board, and its proposals are passed on as "inspiration".

Real participation

The youth group has its own budget line that it decides on itself, a full seat on the board with voting rights and a contact person who helps with the practicalities.

Questions to ask

  • Which decisions can the youth group make on its own?
  • Is there money that the group controls itself?
  • Do young people have a vote where the important decisions are made?

Constructed example 3

Brand or company

Symbolic participation

A clothing company launches a climate campaign with young activists in the advert. The activists are not paid, do not see the finished material before publication and the company's production does not change.

Real participation

The young people taking part are paid, approve how they are portrayed and sit on a reference group with insight into how the company follows up its climate goals, even after the campaign has ended.

Questions to ask

  • Are the young people paid for their time and their name?
  • Can they say no to how they are portrayed?
  • Does anything change in the business, or only in the communication?

Constructed example 4

Public sector or municipality

Symbolic participation

A municipality holds a dialogue evening with young people about a new youth centre. The plan is already finished and the views are summarised in an appendix that nobody returns to. The evening is mentioned in the municipality's press release.

Real participation

Young people are in the working group from the start, help prioritise how part of the budget is used and get feedback that shows what changed after their views.

Questions to ask

  • Was the plan already decided when young people were invited?
  • Are young people's views included in the actual decision documents?
  • How and when does the municipality report back to those who took part?
